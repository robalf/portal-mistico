How to Do a Past Life Regression Safely

By Roberto Rodriguez · September 20, 2026 · 9 min read

Past life regression is a deep relaxation and guided visualization technique that seeks to access memories —real or symbolic, depending on each person's interpretation— of the soul's previous existences. Practiced with care, it can be a revealing introspective experience.

With a professional guide vs. on your own

The most recommended way to experience a regression is with a therapist or guide who has specific training in the subject, who can lead the process safely and help interpret whatever comes up. There are also guided audio recordings designed for practicing at home, useful as a gentler first approach.

Preparation before the session

Choose a quiet space where you won't be interrupted, avoid caffeine or other stimulants beforehand, and spend a few minutes on mindful breathing to slow your mental pace. Setting a clear intention —which pattern or question you'd like to explore— helps focus the experience.

What to expect during the process

A regression usually begins with deep body relaxation, similar to a guided meditation, followed by visualizations that invite you to symbolically "descend" toward older memories. Experiences vary enormously: from vivid, detailed images to simple sensations or emotions with no clear narrative.

Important precautions

If you have a history of severe anxiety, unresolved trauma, or any mental health condition, talk to a health professional before attempting a regression, and consider always doing it with a trained guide. The experience should feel safe at all times; if something feels overwhelming, it's perfectly valid to stop the process.

What does science say about regressions?

From the standpoint of conventional psychology, the experiences reported during a regression are usually explained as the result of a state of deep relaxation that facilitates guided fantasy, cryptomnesia (remembering forgotten information from real sources without identifying its origin), and the brain's heightened suggestibility during hypnotic states. This doesn't invalidate the therapeutic value of the experience — many people gain genuine emotional relief and new perspectives — but it's honest to acknowledge that there is an open debate about whether the images that arise are "literal memories" or symbolic constructions of the mind itself.

After the regression: how to integrate the experience

As important as the session itself is what you do afterward. Many therapists recommend writing down everything you experienced immediately afterward, before the details dissolve the way dreams do. Also take some time to ask yourself what symbolism what you saw holds in relation to your current life — a specific fear, a relationship, a repeated pattern — rather than taking the narrative completely literally. Conscious integration is what turns an interesting experience into a true healing process.

Guided regression vs. clinical hypnosis

It's worth distinguishing between a spiritual regression, guided by an esoteric practitioner focused on the symbolism of the soul, and a clinical hypnotic regression, conducted by a certified psychologist or hypnotherapist with a more structured therapeutic approach. Both approaches can be valuable depending on what you're looking for: the former tends to prioritize open spiritual exploration, while the latter focuses more on resolving a specific symptom or emotional block in your present life.

Frequently asked questions

What is past life regression?

It is a practice that, through deep relaxation or hypnosis, seeks to have a person evoke images or sensations that they interpret as memories of previous existences. At Portal Místico we treat it as an esoteric topic of interest: we explain it to you honestly, without promising results, and offer you free tools to explore your inner world.

Is past life regression scientifically supported?

There is no scientific evidence that past lives are real. Psychology suggests the images arise from imagination, suggestion, and a blending of memories.

What is a regression session usually like?

Usually a facilitator guides you with breathing and relaxation into a state of calm and asks you questions so you can describe what you imagine. Before hiring someone, ask how they work and what method they use.

Is past life regression dangerous?

It can bring on intense emotions or false memories through suggestion, especially if there is anxiety, trauma, or a mental health disorder. That's why it's wise to consult a health professional first if you have doubts.

Can I do a regression by myself at home?

There are guided meditations that promise a regression at home, but without support it's easier to confuse imagination with memory or stir up difficult emotions. If you try it, do so in a quiet setting, with a curious attitude, and without making important decisions based on what you see.
