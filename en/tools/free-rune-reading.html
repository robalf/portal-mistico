What are runes?

Runes are the letters of the ancient Germanic alphabets. The oldest, the Elder Futhark, has 24 signs and gets its name from its first six runes: Fehu, Uruz, Thurisaz, Ansuz, Raidho, and Kenaz. Besides being used for writing, each rune had a name with its own meaning, and today they are used as an oracle to reflect on a situation.

How do you read the three-rune spread?

The first rune speaks of the past, what has brought you here; the second, of the present and the current challenge; and the third, of the future or the likely outcome if you keep going the same way. Read the three together like a story.

Which runes are the most positive?

Sowilo (the Sun), Wunjo (joy), Fehu (wealth), Gebo (the gift), and Dagaz (the dawn) are considered very favorable. Hagalaz, Nauthiz, and Isa speak of pauses and obstacles, but also of what is learned by getting through them.

The 24 runes of the Elder Futhark and their meaning

The Elder Futhark is divided into three groups of eight runes called aettir (singular, aett). Here you'll find each rune with its usual meaning and its reading when the message is a warning. In this spread, the runes always come up in the upright position; the warning column is used as a nuanced reading when the context of your question is difficult.

First aett: Freyr (beginnings and resources)

  • ᚠ Fehu — Wealth, resources, energy in motion. Cautionary nuance: Money or energy that stagnates; not valuing what you have.
  • ᚢ Uruz — Vital strength, health, endurance. Note of caution: Exhaustion or strength misused.
  • ᚦ Thurisaz — Threshold, protection, conflict that forces a decision. Cautionary nuance: Impulsiveness; fighting for the sake of fighting.
  • ᚨ Ansuz — Message, communication, inspiration. Note of caution: Misunderstandings or advice that goes unheard.
  • ᚱ Raidho — Journey, path, the right rhythm. Cautionary nuance: Delays or a path without clear direction.
  • ᚲ Kenaz — Torch, knowledge, creativity. Note of caution: Lack of clarity or ideas that fade.
  • ᚷ Gebo — Gift, reciprocity, balanced exchange. Cautionary nuance: Giving without receiving, or expecting something in return.
  • ᚹ Wunjo — Joy, harmony, shared well-being. Note of caution: Passing disappointment or false peace of mind.

Second aett: Heimdall (challenges and destiny)

  • ᚺ Hagalaz — Hail: an unexpected break that clears the ground. Note of caution: Resisting an inevitable change.
  • ᚾ Nauthiz — Need, limitation, patience in the face of lack. Cautionary nuance: Scarcity that drags on or urgency that is poorly managed.
  • ᛁ Isa — Ice: pause, waiting, freezing. Note of caution: Stagnation that is now asking to move.
  • ᛃ Jera — Harvest, annual cycle, fruit of effort. Cautionary nuance: Results that take time; don't force the timing.
  • ᛇ Eihwaz — Yew: endurance, continuity, a bridge between worlds. Note of caution: Stubbornness that prevents renewal.
  • ᛈ Perthro — Mystery, chance, what has yet to be revealed. Cautionary nuance: Secrets that weigh on you or relying on luck alone.
  • ᛉ Algiz — Protection, defense, connection with the higher. Note of caution: Neglecting your boundaries or feeling exposed.
  • ᛊ Sowilo — Sun, success, clarity, guiding energy. Cautionary nuance: Overconfidence or a brightness that wears you out.

Third aett: Tyr (will and the closing of a cycle)

  • ᛏ Tiwaz — Justice, honor, sacrifice for a cause. Cautionary nuance: Lack of courage or unjust decisions.
  • ᛒ Berkano — Birch: birth, care, renewal. Note of caution: A blocked project or neglect of what is close to you.
  • ᛖ Ehwaz — Horse: teamwork, trust, movement. Cautionary nuance: Distrust or poorly timed progress.
  • ᛗ Mannaz — Humanity, community, oneself and others. Note of caution: Isolation or self-centeredness.
  • ᛚ Laguz — Water: intuition, emotions, flow. Cautionary nuance: Emotional confusion or ignored intuition.
  • ᛜ Ingwaz — Seed, gestation, energy that builds up. Note of caution: Impatience; wanting results before their time.
  • ᛞ Dagaz — Dawn, awakening, clear transformation. Cautionary nuance: Change that resists arriving.
  • ᛟ Othala — Inheritance, home, roots, and belonging. Note of caution: Family conflicts or attachment to what was inherited.

Three-rune spread examples

Question: “How do I approach a job change?” Past: Raidho (you were moving forward at your own pace). Present: Nauthiz (you feel a need or limit pressing on you). Future: Jera (effort bears fruit if you respect the timing). Reading: the change comes after a path already traveled; the current pressure is a stage, and the reward depends on patience.

Question: "What do I need in my relationship?" Past: Gebo (there was mutual giving). Present: Isa (there is a pause or cooling off). Future: Wunjo (harmony returns). Symbolic reading: a period of distance that can be resolved if dialogue is resumed.

How to ask the runes

Open questions work better than yes-or-no ones: "What do I need to see about…?" or "What should I keep in mind in…?" Avoid asking about health, guaranteed money, or other people's decisions. One spread per question: repeating it until you get what you were hoping for defeats the purpose of the exercise.

To keep exploring, you can try the online I Ching or a tarot spread. They are symbolic oracles for reflection, not predictions.

Frequently asked questions

Are reversed runes used?

This spread uses the runes in their upright position, which is how they are read in most simple spreads.

Do the runes come up at random?

Yes. They are shuffled with your device's secure random generator and are not repeated within a single spread.

What is the difference between runes and tarot?

Tarot uses images and 78 cards; runes are 24 older, more austere symbols. Both serve as tools for reflection.
