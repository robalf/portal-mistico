Lunar Nodes Calculator: Your North Node and South Node

The lunar nodes are the two points where the Moon’s orbit crosses the ecliptic. In astrology, the North Node shows where you grow and the South Node what you already master. Enter your birth date to see their signs, the difference between the true and mean node and the dates of your nodal returns.

By Roberto Rodriguez · Updated November 25, 2026

What are the lunar nodes?

The Moon's orbit is tilted about 5 degrees relative to the ecliptic, the Sun's apparent path across the sky. The two orbits cross at two opposite points: the ascending node or North Node (☊), where the Moon moves from south to north of the ecliptic, and the descending node or South Node (☋), where the opposite happens. They aren't celestial bodies but geometric points, yet they play a very real astronomical role: eclipses can only happen when a new moon or full moon falls close to them.

The nodes move slowly backwards through the zodiac and complete a full circle in 18.6 years, so they spend about a year and a half in each sign. In Vedic astrology they're known as Rahu (North) and Ketu (South) and carry enormous weight; in modern Western astrology they're read as the axis of life purpose.

North Node and South Node: the axis of your growth

The two nodes always sit in opposite signs and are read as a path with two ends:

  • South Node: the familiar, the talents that come naturally and the habits it's easy to hide in. Karmic astrology links it with what you learned in the past or, for those who believe in it, in past lives.
  • North Node: what's hard for you but makes you grow. At first it can feel uncomfortable, and over the years it becomes a source of satisfaction and meaning.

The idea isn't to abandon the South Node, but to use its resources to move towards the North. For example, with the North Node in Aquarius and the South Node in Leo, the challenge is to move from personal shine and the need for recognition towards teamwork and shared causes, without losing Leo's creativity.

The six nodal axes

  • North in Aries / South in Libra (or the reverse): independence versus depending on other people's approval.
  • North in Taurus / South in Scorpio: simplicity and stability versus crisis and intensity.
  • North in Gemini / South in Sagittarius: listening and learning versus always having the answer.
  • North in Cancer / South in Capricorn: emotional life and home versus control and status.
  • North in Leo / South in Aquarius: expressing yourself from the heart versus disappearing into the group.
  • North in Virgo / South in Pisces: routine and practical service versus escapism and idealism.

Each axis works both ways: if your North Node is in Libra, the lesson is the reverse of Aries.

True node and mean node

You'll notice the tool shows two positions. The mean node follows a smooth, always retrograde motion, calculated with the formulas of astronomer Jean Meeus. The true node reflects the actual position at each moment: because the Sun and Earth disturb the Moon's orbit, the node wobbles around the mean position by up to about two degrees, and sometimes even moves direct for a few days. Our calculator derives the true node from the actual plane of the Moon's orbit using Astronomy Engine, and we've checked it against NASA's JPL Horizons ephemerides: for January 1, 2000, both give a longitude of 123.95°.

Which should you use? Almost all modern software and most Western astrologers use the true node; some schools, especially Vedic ones, prefer the mean node. It only matters when the node is close to a sign change: in that case, the tool tells you if the two fall in different signs.

Nodal returns and reversals

Every 18.6 years the nodes come back to their position at your birth: this is the nodal return, around ages 18-19, 37, 56 and 74. Halfway through, around 9, 28, 46 and 65, the North Node passes over your natal South Node: this is the nodal reversal. Tradition associates returns with moments of orientation, when people, opportunities or decisions appear that bring you closer to your path, and reversals with reviews when it's worth letting go of old patterns.

From late July 2026 to March 2028, the North Node is moving through Aquarius. That means the people having their nodal return now were born between May 1989 and November 1990 or between December 2007 and August 2009, with their natal North Node in that sign. The 2027 eclipses, such as the annular eclipse of February 6 in Aquarius and the total solar eclipse of August 2 in Leo, fall on that same axis.

How to use your result

Read your South Node sign first and acknowledge its talents without guilt: they're your starting point. Then read your North Node sign and think of one concrete situation in your current life where you could take a step in that direction. If you know your birth time, the house the nodes fall in shows the area of life where this lesson plays out; you can see it in your birth chart. To follow the eclipses that activate your axis, check the esoteric calendar.

Frequently asked questions

What does the North Node mean?

In astrology, the North Node points towards growth: qualities that are hard at first but bring meaning and satisfaction as you develop them.

Is the South Node always in the opposite sign?

Yes. The two nodes are always exactly 180 degrees apart, so if you know one you know the other.

Should I use the true node or the mean node?

Most Western astrologers use the true node, which is the actual position. The mean node is an average. The result only changes when the node is near a sign change.

How often do the nodes change sign?

About every year and a half. They travel the whole zodiac, moving backwards, in 18.6 years.

How are the nodes related to eclipses?

Eclipses only happen when a new or full moon falls near the nodes, because only then do the Sun, Earth and Moon truly line up.
